## PART 2 — WRITING (40 p.)

**Grilla orientativa (propia; la cátedra no publica la suya):**

| Criterio | Puntos |
|---|---|
| Cumple la consigna (opinion essay): ¶1 tema + **tu opinión**; ¶2 y ¶3 razones con ejemplos o datos; ¶4 conclusión que reafirma la opinión con otras palabras | 10 |
| Organización: 4 párrafos, *topic sentences*, conectores | 10 |
| Gramática: tiempos, concordancia, pasiva y modales bien usados | 10 |
| Vocabulario académico, sin repetir, con nominalización | 10 |

> **Se toma UN solo lado y NO se menciona el otro.** Según un alumno que rindió el final, la
> cátedra tacha lo que se escribe del lado contrario. Acá no va el "On the other hand…" del
> *for and against essay* de los simulacros 1 y 2.
> **Usá las ideas del texto parafraseadas; no inventes argumentos propios complejos ni copies
> oraciones enteras.** El texto trae cuatro ideas: (1) los algoritmos amplifican lo falso;
> (2) las plataformas ganan con esa atención y tienen herramientas que los usuarios no
> tienen; (3) es imposible revisar todo (en contra); (4) riesgo de censura (en contra). Quien
> está **de acuerdo** usa 1 y 2; quien está **en desacuerdo** usa 3 y 4.

**Esquema posible (de acuerdo):**
1. *Intro + opinión:* false posts reach millions; I strongly agree that companies should be responsible.
2. *Razón 1:* algorithms maximise engagement and spread false stories (ejemplo: vaccine claim vs. correction).
3. *Razón 2:* companies profit from attention and own the tools (fact-checkers, labels, reach reduction).
4. *Conclusión:* reafirma la opinión con otras palabras.

**Modelo (~250 palabras, propio, un solo lado):**

> Every day, millions of false posts reach social media users, from invented health advice
> to fake stories about elections. I strongly agree that the companies behind these
> platforms should be held responsible for the misinformation published on them.
>
> First of all, platforms do not simply host false content; they actively spread it. Their
> recommendation algorithms are designed to keep users engaged, and shocking or untrue
> stories usually attract more reactions than truthful ones. As a result, a false claim about
> a vaccine can be shown to far more people than the correction that follows it. Since the
> system itself favours misinformation, those who designed it must answer for the
> consequences.
>
> Secondly, the companies are in a much better position than ordinary users to solve the
> problem. They earn money from the advertising linked to our attention, and they own tools
> that users do not have: teams of fact-checkers, warning labels and the technical power to
> reduce the reach of a harmful post. A user cannot verify every message he or she reads,
> but a company with these resources can act. Legal responsibility would give it a real
> reason to do so.
>
> In conclusion, social media companies profit from the attention that false stories attract
> and control the technology that spreads them. For these reasons, I firmly believe that they
> ought to be held accountable for the misinformation on their platforms.

## ORAL

Argumentos posibles para la moción *"social media platforms should verify the identity of
every user"* (for/against):

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Anonymous accounts spread misinformation and harassment without consequences. | Activists and whistle-blowers in repressive countries need anonymity. |
| Verified users can be held accountable when they break the rules. | Collecting identity documents creates a huge database that can be hacked. |
| Bots and fake profiles would become much harder to create. | It excludes people without official documents and is expensive for small platforms. |

Si el examinador prefiere formato *problem/solution*: *Problem 1:* fake accounts spread
misinformation → *solution:* verification only for accounts with large audiences → *result:*
fewer viral falsehoods without removing anonymity for everyone. *Problem 2:* user data could
leak → *solution:* verification by a third party that keeps no copies → *result:* less risk.
