Daniel: Good morning, everyone. Today I'd like to talk about something most of us never think about when we upload a photo, watch a video or ask a chatbot a question. And that something is water.
Daniel: Every online service runs on servers, and servers are kept in enormous buildings called data centres. Thousands of machines working day and night produce a great deal of heat, and if that heat is not removed, the equipment simply fails. The most common way to remove it is through evaporative cooling. Water absorbs the heat and then evaporates into the air, which means that it is lost. It does not return to the river or to the water supply.
Daniel: How much water are we talking about? Well, estimates vary, but a medium-sized data centre can use about one point five million litres of water a day. That's one point five million. To put that figure into perspective, it is roughly what a town of ten thousand people consumes in a single day.
Daniel: Artificial intelligence has made the situation more visible. Researchers have calculated that a conversation of twenty to fifty questions with a chatbot may use about one bottle of water, around half a litre, once you include the water needed to cool the servers and to generate the electricity. One bottle doesn't sound like much, but multiply it by hundreds of millions of users.
Daniel: Now, the problem is not the same everywhere. It is much more serious in dry regions, where water is already scarce and where local communities and farmers compete for the same resource. And unfortunately, many data centres have been built precisely in those places, because land and energy are cheap there.
Daniel: So what are companies doing about it? Three things, mainly. First, some of them are building new data centres in cold countries, such as Finland or Sweden, where the outside air can do much of the cooling for free. Second, some centres now use recycled water, that is, treated waste water, instead of drinking water. And third, a few projects reuse the heat itself. In Helsinki, for example, the waste heat from a data centre is used to warm homes nearby through the city's heating network. Instead of being a problem, the heat becomes a resource.
