## PART 2 — WRITING (40 p.)

Tipo de ensayo: **for and against** (balanceado; la opinión recién en la conclusión; sin
*I think* antes del último párrafo). **Usá las ideas del texto parafraseadas; no inventes
argumentos propios complejos ni copies oraciones enteras.**

**Grilla orientativa (propia; la cátedra no publica la suya):**

| Criterio | Puntos |
|---|---|
| Cumple la consigna: argumentos a favor **y** en contra del texto, sin opinión hasta el final | 10 |
| Organización: 4 párrafos, *topic sentences*, conectores | 10 |
| Gramática: tiempos, concordancia, pasiva y modales bien usados | 10 |
| Vocabulario académico, sin repetir, con paráfrasis del texto (sin copiar oraciones) | 10 |

**Ideas del texto que hay que usar (3-4):**
1. A favor: casi no emite CO2 al operar (y su ciclo de vida es comparable al eólico).
2. A favor: produce día y noche, con cualquier clima: estabiliza la red (complemento de solar y eólica).
3. En contra: caras y lentas de construir (más de una década, varias veces el presupuesto), mientras las emisiones deben bajar ya.
4. En contra: residuos radiactivos por miles de años, sin depósito permanente en la mayoría de los países.

**Esquema:** ¶1 introducción sin opinión → ¶2 a favor (ideas 1 y 2) → ¶3 en contra (ideas 3 y 4) → ¶4 conclusión con opinión o resumen balanceado.

**Modelo (~250 palabras, propio):**

> Climate change obliges countries to replace fossil fuels with low-carbon sources of
> electricity, and nuclear power is one of the most debated options. Is it really the
> best answer? There are good arguments on both sides.
>
> On the one hand, nuclear stations release hardly any carbon dioxide while they work,
> and over their whole life their emissions per unit of electricity are similar to those
> of a wind farm. In addition, they do not depend on the weather, so they supply power at
> night and in every season. This steady output keeps the grid stable when demand rises,
> which is why many experts consider nuclear energy a good partner for solar and wind
> power.
>
> On the other hand, there are serious drawbacks. Nuclear plants are costly and slow to
> finish: many projects have needed more than ten years and have ended up costing several
> times their original budget, whereas wind and solar farms are ready much sooner. Since
> emissions have to fall quickly in this decade, such delays are a problem. Moreover, the
> used fuel stays radioactive for thousands of years, and most countries have not decided
> where to keep it, so future generations inherit the burden.
>
> In conclusion, nuclear power offers clean and reliable electricity, but it is slow,
> expensive and produces dangerous waste. In my opinion, it can contribute to the fight
> against climate change, but it is not the best solution on its own; it should work
> together with renewable energy.
