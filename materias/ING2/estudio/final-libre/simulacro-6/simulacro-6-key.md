**Question 1 (5 × 1 p.)**

    1) 1924      2) ECoG      3) 96 / ninety-six      4) coffee      5) scarring

- 1: trampa: *1929*. "Hans Berger published his first article … in nineteen twenty-nine,
  but he had made the first human **recording** five years earlier, in **nineteen
  twenty-four**." La nota pregunta por la grabación.
- 2: trampa: *EEG* (sensores en el cuero cabelludo: "sensors on the scalp. That is the
  EEG"). Los que están **debajo del cráneo, sobre el cerebro** son "ECoG: E, C, O, G".
  También vale *electrocorticography*.
- 3: trampa: *100*. "a ten-by-ten grid of needles. That would be a hundred, but four
  positions are empty, so it has **ninety-six** electrodes."
- 4: trampa: *water*. "Many reports say she drank water. **In fact**, it was **coffee**."
- 5: trampa: *infection*. "People worry about infection, and surgeons control that quite
  well. **The real problem is scarring.**" También vale *scar*.

**Question 2 (3 × 4 p.)**

a) A supply chain attack is an attack in which, instead of attacking a well-defended
target directly, the attacker poisons a component (a library) that the victims already
trust, so that the compromised update travels automatically to every project that
depends on it and the victims install it themselves.

b) **Contradicted.** Raymond says that if thousands of people can read the code, someone
will notice every flaw. But Heartbleed, a serious flaw in OpenSSL (a library that protects
a large share of the world's web traffic), went unnoticed for about two years: the
project was maintained by a handful of mostly volunteer developers with very little
funding, and many programmers used the code but hardly anyone studied it.

c) The author's answer is: too often, a few tired (unpaid) volunteers. Companies that earn
billions from open source treat it as a free resource, and the next attack is a question
of when, not if; unless they share the cost (money, engineers' time, responsibility), the
foundations of the digital world will remain fragile.

Criterio: en la b), "Contradicted" sin dato vale como máximo 2 p. En la c), nombrar a los
voluntarios sin el pedido de compartir el costo vale 2 p.

**Question 10 — Cloze (6 × 0,5 p.)**

    1) because      2) whereas      3) which      4) where      5) by      6) However

Sobran: **unless** y **until**.

- 1 y 2: si los cambiás, *whereas* queda sin sentido ("difficult whereas…") y *because*
  no marca contraste entre modelos grandes y dispositivos limitados.
- 3: *which* y no *where*: *pruning, which removes…* (el relativo es sujeto de *removes*).
- 4: *where* (≈ *in which*): la destilación como proceso donde el modelo pequeño aprende.
  *Which* no va porque *a small student model learns* ya tiene sujeto.
- 6: *However* + coma abre la oración que contrasta con el resultado de la cuantización.

## PART 2 — WRITING (40 p.)

Tipo de ensayo: **opinion essay** ("Do you agree or disagree? Give reasons and examples").
Estructura (handout de Bettucci): ¶1 introduce el tema y da **tu** opinión; ¶2 y ¶3 razones
con ejemplos o datos que la apoyan; ¶4 conclusión que reafirma la opinión con otras palabras.

> **Se toma UN solo lado y NO se menciona el otro** (según un alumno que rindió el final,
> la cátedra tacha lo que se escribe del otro lado). El modelo de abajo lo cumple: no
> aparece ni una idea de los críticos.
>
> **Usá las ideas del texto parafraseadas; no inventes argumentos propios complejos ni
> copies oraciones enteras.**

**Grilla orientativa (propia; la cátedra no publica la suya):**

| Criterio | Puntos |
|---|---|
| Cumple la consigna: una sola postura clara, con razones y ejemplos del texto | 10 |
| Organización: 4 párrafos (opinión, razón 1, razón 2, conclusión), conectores | 10 |
| Gramática: tiempos, concordancia, pasiva y modales bien usados | 10 |
| Vocabulario académico, sin repetir, con paráfrasis del texto (sin copiar oraciones) | 10 |

**Ideas del texto (3-4):**
1. A favor: aprender a programar enseña a dividir un problema en pasos y a buscar errores con paciencia, útil en cualquier materia (ejemplo: el niño que hace cruzar un laberinto a un personaje; Inglaterra, obligatorio desde 2014).
2. A favor: los chicos pasan de usuarios a creadores de tecnología y se preparan para un mercado laboral que pide habilidades digitales.
3. En contra: le quita tiempo a lectura, escritura y matemática.
4. En contra: muchos docentes de primaria no tienen formación.

Quien elige **estar de acuerdo** usa las ideas 1 y 2; quien elige **no estarlo** usa las 3 y 4
(con ejemplos propios simples) y no menciona las otras.

**Modelo (~250 palabras, propio, postura: de acuerdo):**

> I strongly agree that programming should be a compulsory subject in primary schools.
> Computers are present in almost every profession, so children should learn how they
> work from an early age, as has been the case in England since 2014.
>
> First of all, learning to program develops habits of mind that help in every subject.
> To make a character cross a maze, for example, a child must divide the task into small
> steps, try them out, find the errors and correct them patiently until the program
> works. This way of tackling problems is just as useful in mathematics, science or
> writing a story, so programming benefits the whole education of a pupil.
>
> Moreover, programming turns children from simple users of technology into creators of
> it. A pupil who understands how an app or a website is built does not see these tools
> as magic and is able to imagine new ones. This knowledge is also valuable for the
> future, since employers increasingly look for digital skills and young people who start
> early will be better prepared to find a job.
>
> In conclusion, making programming compulsory in primary school gives every child the
> chance to learn how to solve problems and to become a creator of technology. For these
> reasons, I firmly believe that it should be part of the basic curriculum.
